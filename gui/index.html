Quick guide

What this program does

It docks small molecules into a protein with AutoDock Vina. Before it docks your compounds, it checks that the settings can reproduce a known answer (re-docking of the crystal ligand). Then it ranks poses using several criteria, not only the Vina score.

Five steps

  1. Protein: type a PDB ID (or upload a file) and press Load. If there are several chains, a suggestion is shown with its reason. You confirm or change it.
  2. Compounds: paste SMILES, or upload MOL / MOL2 / SDF / SMI / CSV files. Check the structure drawings and accept or remove warnings.
  3. Validate & box: choose how the box is defined (co-crystal ligand, click residues, pocket detection, or manual coordinates) and preview it in 3D.
  4. Run: press Start. A small test takes minutes; large libraries take longer. Higher exhaustiveness is slower and more thorough.
  5. Results: click a compound to see its poses in 3D and the 2D interaction diagram. Export tables, poses and images.

How to read the result

  • Vina affinity (kcal/mol): more negative is better, but it is an estimate, not a measured binding energy. Do not compare across different proteins.
  • "Validation passed" means redocking gave RMSD under the limit. It supports, but does not prove, that docking is reliable for your compounds.
  • Composite score and shortlist are a heuristic to help human review. Look at the poses before trusting them.

If something goes wrong

  • Validation failed: check the chain choice and the box, or use another structure. Do not trust results from a failed validation.
  • Structure did not download: check the internet, or upload the .pdb file.
  • A compound is rejected: fix the structure (single molecule, valid atoms) and add it again.
  • Anything unclear: open Run > Technical log and send it to your instructor.

Your files stay on this computer. Runs are saved in AbdulwahhabVinaWorkflow\runs in your user folder. Internet is only used to download PDB files and to check for updates.
